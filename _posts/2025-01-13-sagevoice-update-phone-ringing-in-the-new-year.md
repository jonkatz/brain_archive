## What is SageVoice doing again? Focus continues to shift as I find traction.

**Original mission statement:** _SageVoice uses AI-enabled telephony to augment the growing elderly caregiver gap._

+30° Pivot. First I pivoted away from a direct-to-consumer model, because it was clear that I needed institutional support to initiate usage.

+30° Pivot.  I’m starting to get encouraging traction with folks in academia and medicine who are looking to reach patients or subjects at scale for a variety of reasons. In the interest of not-failing, I’m shifting focus to follow that demand.

**Mission statement for current focus:** _SageVoice uses AI-enabled telephony to provide structured conversation to ‘health-ish’ institutions struggling with scaled, 2-way personal communications._

I’m actually pretty stoked about it, but as you’ll see from the story below I’m still feeling the pull of what I set out to do.

## Quick Story

While I was on vacation. I got a call from a number I didn’t recognize and they left a voicemail.  It was from one of the user testers who hadn’t used the product for several weeks. It was from a former user-tester, the woman who “hates AI” who came around after 3 calls.

Here is the bulk of the voicemail transcript:

_…I tried to reach Sage. I'm kind of in trouble. I'm by myself and ….. and so I I thought I would try to call Sage. And try to get some support there, but the number that I have I can't reach her. So, can you please call me and give me the correct number? The number I have for her is ###-###-####  but as I said, I can't reach her. I I really need somebody to talk to. I'm really I'm having a really hard time…._

She had the wrong number for Sage. I called her back immediately, because I was concerned and did not want to trust a mental health emergency on my half-baked creation. I tried to personally listen and give her what she was looking for from Sage.  She was incredibly articulate about her situation, but was also very clear that she didn’t really want to talk to me: she wanted to talk to Sage.

Going into this new year, I’m sad because it’s clear I was right that bots can help fill a gap (or even improve on what humans do) to solve this very real problem of loneliness. But I couldn’t figure out the lever to get people to try it.  My hope is that if I can get traction elsewhere, I can revisit this or enough time will have passed that the onboarding problem will be less severe.

## 1st 6 months reflection

Since it’s the start of a new year, I thought I’d share what I’ve learned and done over the last 6 months I’ve been doing this in stat form:

- **>135 people met with.** Industry experts, target customers, user testers, potential cofounders, and VCs.

  - Flyers
  - Social network appeals
  - Networking events
  - **The best insights came from the many amazing people who helped with advice or putting me in touch. Thanks to many of YOU.**

- 6 landing pages, 2 websites (3rd one this week)
- **2 moments of “this might be it”!, where it really wasn't “it” 1 moment that still might be “it”.**
- > 8 paid ad tests
- **Spent about $6k** ($3k on ad tests)
- **$0 revenue**
- **Proof both that the original offering was valuable and that it had customer acquisition issues that make it non-viable.**
- 3 homemade, working versions of the product (story telling, elderly monitoring and now: “ information exchange”)
- Limited applications to 3 incubators after 3 rejection. 0 govt funding applications. Right to focus 100% on finding the right customer with the right problem before I worry about funding?

I’ve been updating you with my learnings as they come in, reflecting on this, I made the classic mistake and “overbuilt” before finding the right problem to solve.

At times I’ve felt like I’ve been wandering in the desert, but I’m currently very optimistic that I’m making progress. I feel like I’m honing in on the customers and problems that are the best fit for my approach and DNA.
